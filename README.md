# ML_project
This project was done for my ML course at TU Dortmund during the second semester of my International Master Degree in Particle Physics (IMAPP).

Electroencephalogram (EEG) signals are electrical signals of the brain recorded by electrodes placed in various positions on the scalp. They carry information about brain activity; however, extracting and interpreting that information is a challenge because of their complexity. With the rising popularity of deep learning techniques, recent efforts have focused on using deep learning to interpret EEG signals. The hope is that a deep learning network can recognize and extract patterns from the complicated signals. The goal of this project was a classification of EEG signals by the specific digit shown to the subject while recording the signal. The digit used shown to the subject were taken from the MNIST dataset, a database of images of handwritten digits numbered 0-9 (2). It is a very popular dataset to test machine learning methods for image recognition tasks, and is referenced in many tutorials on the topic. Stimuli like image of digits are very netrual and as a consequence hard to recognize and classify with respect to stimuli who require different body movements or a high brain activity, but the applications are endless. Classification of images looked at and imagined by the subject, offers a starting point for the development of machine learning methods to interpret brain signals.

In this study, we explored CNN models to classify the EEG signals associated with the visually evoked digits. The dataset were taken from the MindBigData repository, specifically focusing on ”The MNIST of Brain Digits” and ”The Visual MNIST of Brain Digits.” Through a comprehensive analysis and experimentation with different models and preprocessing techniques, we aimed to improve the classification accuracy of EEG signals. The original aim of precisely classifying the different signal by their respective digit proved to be out of our reach given the recources available and/or intrinsic limitations of the datasets. Our focus then shifted to a binary classification problem. Since the Muse and Muse2 dataset contained signals without a visual digit stimulation, we tried to classify the data samples in digit or non digit. Initially, we recreated the 1D CNN model from Das et al., for the Muse dataet with font digits, which yielded an accuracy of approximately 90% on both training and validation sets. We then generalized this approach to ”The Visual MNIST of Brain Digits” dataset, utilizing the Muse2 subdataset, which deals with handwritten digits instead. For which the accuracy dropped to to 65% on the validation. Hyperparameter optimization was then performed by separately optimizing preprocessing parameters and model hyperparameters, we identified configurations that enhanced model performance. The final optimized model, incorporating adjustments in convolutional layers, f ilter sizes, and dense layer configurations, achieved a validation accuracy of 69% on both validation and test dataset. Even though the initial goal of the digit classification was not reached ourtstudy assessed the capability of a CNN network to classify non trivial EEG signals giving promising results for further explorations on the matter.
